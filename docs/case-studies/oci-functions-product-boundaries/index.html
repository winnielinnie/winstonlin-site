Customer Architecture Questions

Turned hard customer questions into clearer architecture guidance, support answers, and product follow-up.

OCI FunctionsProduct contribution
01 The problem02 My contribution03 The decisions04 Where it landed

The problem

Many high-value Functions questions were not clean feature requests. They were asks about extreme scale, Lambda migration, large-memory ingestion, enterprise governance, cold starts, IAM, adjacent services, and whether a pattern was supportable at all.

My contribution

Separated the immediate customer answer from the product follow-up: what is supported today, what requires an architecture change, what belongs in service limits, and what should influence roadmap direction.

Gave clear support answers around modified runtimes and framework assumptions while preserving the signal that migration customers expected simpler deployment and runtime ergonomics.

Helped field and engineering reason through large-scale and enterprise scenarios such as AI-generated function fleets, high-throughput file ingestion, governance controls, capacity-constrained environments, and low-latency execution.

The decisions

The risky move would have been to answer every question as either yes, no, or file a ticket. The useful work was deciding which questions exposed product gaps, adjacent-service dependencies, support risks, or future workload patterns.

Customer trust depended on being precise about today's platform boundaries while still converting repeated friction into roadmap, docs, examples, or service-limit planning.

Where it landed

Gave customer and field teams clearer answers without over-promising unsupported surfaces.

Turned escalation patterns into product inputs around migration readiness, supportability, service limits, operational confidence, and future serverless workload shape.
